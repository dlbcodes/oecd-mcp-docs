# Example questions

Once [connected](/guide/getting-started), ask your assistant these in plain
language — including analytical questions that draw on several datasets at once.
The server finds each dataset, resolves the series, and cites every figure.

## Single indicators

> How has real GDP per capita in the euro area changed since 2010, and which
> member states have converged toward the average?

> What's the harmonised unemployment rate across France, Germany, Italy and
> Spain, and what's the most recent month reported for each?

## Across several datasets

These pull from multiple OECD datasets in one conversation:

> Compare Ireland's GDP-per-capita growth with its population growth over the
> past two decades — is the per-capita gain real or a denominator effect?

> For the EU Cohesion Fund countries, show tertiary education enrolment
> alongside GDP per capita — are the fastest-converging economies also the ones
> expanding higher education?

> How does population ageing across Italy, Germany and Japan line up with their
> labour-force participation rates?

## When the data isn't there

The part that matters most if you'll be quoted — ask for something OECD doesn't
publish, and it tells you instead of inventing a figure:

> What's the OECD Composite Leading Indicator for Portugal?

It explains the headline CLI isn't published for Portugal (and points to related
confidence indicators that _are_) — rather than fabricating values.

> Give me Kenya's GDP per capita from the OECD.

It tells you Kenya isn't OECD-covered, instead of producing a made-up series.

## Reading the answers

Every data answer carries a **table** of figures, the **unit** and how it's
measured (e.g. "current PPP dollars per person" — not euros, not
inflation-adjusted), a **Data Explorer link** to open and download the exact
series on the OECD's own site, and the exact **series key**. Treat those as your
citation: when you quote a number, quote its unit and link too.
